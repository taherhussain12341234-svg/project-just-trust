    Sources & Credits

    Where the facts come from

    Made for curious kids. Facts and coordinates come from the public sources below. Please check them if you use this in a classroom.

    • Apollo 11, 12 and 15: NASA, Apollo Lunar Surface Journal and mission reports, nasa.gov.
    • Landing-site coordinates and orbit photos: NASA / Goddard / Arizona State University, Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Camera, lroc.asu.edu.
    • Surveyor 3: NASA Science; camera tests: NASA Technical Reports Server; collectSPACE. The camera is on loan to the Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum.
    • Moon laser mirrors: Eos, “Seeing the Light” and LROC.
    • Photos of the Apollo 15 buggy, the Apollo 11 lunar module Eagle in lunar orbit, the Apollo 12 lunar module Intrepid, Surveyor 3, Perseverance, Ingenuity and a sample tube: NASA. The sample-tube picture is a frame from NASA test footage made on Earth, not a photo taken on Mars. Keep NASA’s original captions with them.

    More credits

    • Mars craft and Jezero Crater: NASA / JPL-Caltech and ESA, science.nasa.gov/mars. Ingenuity: Smithsonian Air & Space.
    • Mars Sample Return status (January 2026): The Register and Live Science. Plans can change, so check NASA.
    • Photos: the pictures of each craft are NASA images built into this page. If a photo cannot be shown, a drawing made for this guide appears instead, marked “Illustration”. Check the NASA media guidelines before reusing any photo.
    • Map: drawn in the style of lunar and Martian topographic maps. Shapes are illustrations, not real elevation data, and marker positions are approximate. Surveyor 3 and Apollo 12 are only about 600 ft apart, so they are drawn apart.
    • 3D Lab: built with three.js (MIT license), loaded from cdnjs and jsDelivr when you open the tab. The 3D models are simplified drawings made for this guide, not exact engineering models.
    • Rocket Balancer: a simplified teaching estimate, not an official NASA number.
    • Fonts: Outfit and Nunito Sans from Google Fonts (SIL Open Font License). NASA does not endorse this site.
